On Saturday, October 17, I got a scary message. Half of my Claude API credits were gone. A second email said all of them were gone. A third said my API access had been blocked because I had used up my credits. When I looked at the console, I saw that someone had been using one of my API keys: first Fable 5.1, then, after that got rate limited, Opus 5. It took a few minutes to burn through everything.

![API usage on October 17 showing a spike of 1,670,579 input tokens across Opus 5 and Fable 5.1, with 22,981 output tokens.](/static/images/the-future-of-work/api-usage.jpg)

My first thought was that I had been hacked. I spent the whole morning checking my computer and home network, with Codex helping me look. Nothing. No suspicious logs, and the key itself didn't even show up where I expected it. It was only about seven euros, but I was still a bit panicky. Then OpenCode emailed me: their systems had been compromised and my API key might have been exposed. Half an hour later a second email confirmed that my Anthropic key was affected.[^opencode-incident] So I wasn't hacked, but I did want to report it to Anthropic. If anyone was going to say I had misused my key, I wanted them to know where the leak came from.

Anthropic keeps telling us AI will automate customer support, so I expected the best automated support experience I had ever seen. The console has a help button that opens an AI chatbot. First I had to accept the conditions of use. Then it gave me a menu: technical issues, usage limits, subscription, billing, feature requests. A compromised API key wasn't on the list, so I picked one at random and was finally allowed to type. The bot said it would hand me over to a human. That was October 17. I heard nothing until October 21, and I haven't heard back about my refund since.

Meanwhile I found a security email address and wrote a short message. The reply was automated, but clearly not written by an AI: vulnerability? Use this link. Fraud? Email this address. Model safety? Email that one. In other words, the company with some of the smartest models in the world asked me, the customer, to do the triage for them.

![Anthropic's automated security email directing customers to different channels for vulnerabilities, fraud, model safety, compliance, and other support requests.](/static/images/the-future-of-work/security-triage.jpg)

OpenCode did much better. Their co-founder Dax answered quickly, said others had already been refunded, and offered to refund me too. I said I'd wait for Anthropic first. That felt personal and helpful, but it works because OpenCode is small. It doesn't scale, and scaling is exactly what AI is supposed to fix.

Anthropic runs its support agent on Salesforce[^fin-ownership] and says it resolves about half of incoming questions.[^anthropic-support] I'd like to know what happens to the other half. Someone has to own those cases and keep the customer informed. Companies that have scaled support with humans for decades know how hard that is, and they are the ones struggling to do it with AI. If Anthropic can't make it work for its own customers, I'm not sure what that says for everyone else. Coding seems to scale for them, but what about finance or support? I suspect the honest answer is that they haven't figured it out either.

In the end, it looks like I got my credits back, and my account works again. Nobody told me, but maybe that notification is still waiting for a human in the loop to review and approve it.

[^opencode-incident]: See the Hacker News discussions of [OpenCode’s breach announcement](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50133401) and the [subsequent OpenCode and OpenAI refund update](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50148946).

[^anthropic-support]: See the interview [Why Anthropic chose Fin to transform their customer support](https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcasts/why-anthropic-chose-fin-to-transform-their-customer-support) and Fin’s case study [Build vs buy: Why Anthropic chose Fin](https://fin.ai/customers/anthropic).

[^fin-ownership]: Salesforce [completed its acquisition of Fin, formerly Intercom, on September 10, 2026](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/09/10/salesforce-completes-acquisition-of-fin/).
