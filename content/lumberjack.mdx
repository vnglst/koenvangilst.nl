Jack lost his job as a software engineer to AI. To provide for his young family, he takes a job as a lumberjack in Finland. The pay is good and the work is engaging. It's good to be out in nature and work with his hands. He didn't ask what the land is for. With two kids, you don't ask.

Wow, this is incredible. People have been sharing beautiful games, videos, and even songs they created with Fable 5.2 lately, so I gave it a shot myself this weekend. Together with my 11-year-old son, we created a game called [Lumber Jack](https://lumber-jack.koenvangilst.nl), and it exceeded all our expectations. The graphics are beautiful, exactly as we wanted them, and the gameplay is engaging and surprising.

Of course, we didn't stop at the already impressive visuals. We turned the idea into a small automation game in plain JavaScript. You start by cutting trees yourself, then build a logging village, hire more jacks, and automate the work. As the settlement grows, so does the forest clearance - and eventually you discover what the land was being cleared for.

The game is inspired by my memories of *Warcraft* (1994), *Stardew Valley*, the wonderful book *Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow* by Gabrielle Zevin, and a little bit of Monty Python. It is a strange combination, but somehow it all fits: resource management, a cozy village, a story about making things together, and just enough absurdity.

It was made as a family project, but the result feels like a real game. There is a satisfying loop of gathering, building, and automating, with enough story underneath to make you wonder whether Jack should really keep expanding the operation.
